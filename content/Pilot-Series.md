**Program:** [BRIDGE Project](BRIDGE-Project) (Link Program) · **Series:** 0–4 · **Timeline:** Canon

Mech pilots are grouped into **series** according to how and when they were brought into the program.

## Overview

| Series | Who | Selection | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **[Series 0](#series-0)** | Pilots from before the Link Program | Training | Some exceptional outliers |
| **[Series 1](#series-1)** | Link Program scientists | None | Average aptitude; no exceptional pilots |
| **[Series 2](#series-2)** | Paid, expendable volunteers of all ages | Mass testing | Tens of thousands; most died |
| **[Series 3](#series-3)** | Genetically engineered clones | Engineered | Fewest and best pilots |
| **[Series 4](#series-4)** | Volunteers chosen by aptitude test | Aptitude testing | Piloting becomes popular |

Series 3 produced the fewest and best pilots. The only exceptions are outliers from Series 0, 2 and 4.

## Aptitude

**Aptitude** is a measure of how well a person can link with a mech.

* It depends heavily on **brain elasticity**, especially at the first connection, so younger people score higher on average.
* It changes as the brain develops, but after a person's first link connection it declines **much more slowly**.
* With training it can **stabilise or even improve**.

The aptitude tester used in Series 4 came out of the Series 2 data.

## Series 0

Every pilot from before the Link Program, including the trained crews of the [first-generation mechs](First-Generation-Mechs).

## Series 1

The scientists of the Link Program who piloted [Generation 1](BRIDGE-Mechs#generation-1) machines. All were adults, and none were tested for aptitude because no test existed yet. Later testing put them at **average aptitude**, far below any true pilot.

The only notable Series 1 pilot is [the Engineer](The-Engineer), who died piloting [G2M01](BRIDGE-Mechs#lpg2-m01).

## Series 2

### Purpose

Created after the government takeover to find out **why some people have higher aptitude than others**. It was strictly a large research dataset, and no survivor became a pilot because of it.

### Subjects

Mostly orphans, the homeless, and people with nothing left to lose or looking for money. Subjects of **all ages** were paid a large sum for every successful test. Series 2 is the second-largest series, with subjects numbering in the **tens of thousands**.

### Cost

More than half of Series 2 died, either at their first connection or on return visits, and most died after a single test. The governments covered up every death. Survivors were bound by non-disclosure agreements, and those who spoke out after release were silenced. The sheer number of disappearances is what brought [HAS](HAS) into the picture.

### Findings

Series 2 established the link between **brain elasticity** and aptitude, and that younger subjects make better pilots.

### Outliers

A few subjects performed incredibly well. Samples taken from them became the genetic templates for the [Series 3 clones](Series-3-Clones). Most of the best returned so many times that the tests eventually killed them, but a few stopped, and some later joined Series 4.

## Series 3

Genetically engineered clones of the best Series 2 pilots, raised inside the program's facilities. It is the smallest series and took the most effort to produce. The surviving clones were extremely valuable and were used mainly for mech tests.

Series 3 ended after the death of [Daniel](Daniel) (S39) in [BRIDGE-Fault](BRIDGE-Fault). See [Series 3 Clones](Series-3-Clones).

## Series 4

By Series 4 the program had gained traction and lost much of its secrecy. It moved away from expensive, slow cloning and used an **aptitude tester** to find high-value volunteers.

* Wanting to be a pilot is common.
* Aptitude testing becomes **required** in many places.
* Piloting is still a choice, but most people who test well join, and most who test badly wish they could.

Some Series 2 outliers who survived eventually became part of Series 4.
