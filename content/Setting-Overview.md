## Premise

The universe is a **retrofuture**: history runs as we know it until the mid-1970s and then diverges. The Cold War is fought as a [science race](Science-Race) rather than an arms race. The space race never really ends, the USSR never dissolves, and the public stays fascinated by spaceflight after Apollo. By the early 1980s humanity has net-positive fusion, a space warship, and boots on Mars.

In **September 1984**, the Europa probe *Aurora 2* sights an alien fleet near Jupiter. The fleet belongs to [the Remnants](The-Remnants), refugees whose homeworld was destroyed by [the Conglomerate](The-Conglomerate). After a short, one-sided clash the Remnants surrender, and within months the two species form the [Remnant-Human Alliance](Remnant-Human-Alliance). The Remnants warn that the Conglomerate will eventually come for Earth. See [First Contact](First-Contact).

The alliance starts a [second industrial age](Second-Industrial-Age), building Remnant-Human technology for the coming war. Decades later a team watching through a [solar gravitational lens](Solar-Gravitational-Lens) confirms the Conglomerate fleet is on its way, and the [Conglomerate War](Conglomerate-War) begins.

The Conglomerate expected to crush Earth the way it crushed the Remnants. The alliance turns out to be much stronger than that, so the Conglomerate offers a compromise: join, fight the greater threat, and give up Earth's resources. **History branches here.** See [Timelines](Timelines).

## Tech level

Anything that is physically possible: nuclear fusion, solar gravitational lenses, laser terraforming, asteroid and extraplanetary mining. Everyday human life still looks and feels like the 1980s. The big leaps are in **military technology, weapons, spaceflight and power generation**. See [Technology](Technology).

## Tone

The universe is grounded and mechanical. Human machines feel heavy and practical. The Conglomerate's bio-mechanical technology and [the Ultimate Threat](The-disease) are monstrous and alien. War is never glorious: victories are short and paid for dearly.
